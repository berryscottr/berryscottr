Does it hinge on the cut-off dates?

We moved both ends of the year, 946 ways

A result can look good or bad because of where its measurement starts and ends. So we re-scored the holdout year with every start date up to a month earlier or later and every end date up to a month earlier. The data ends on Nov 5, 2026, so the end can't move later.

  • Every window: −0.30 to +0.74, and 86% of them are positive.
  • ±1 day on both ends: 0.06 to 0.21.
  • No choice of dates lifts the year near the 1.0 planning level. The weakness is real, not a cut-off artefact.
  • Why boundaries matter less than you'd think: profit and loss is marked to market every day, so a cut-off only changes which days count. It can't slice a trade's profit in half.

Two holdout numbers, and why. The official run starts with an empty book on Nov 1 and scores 0.16. An account already running carries positions opened in October into November and scores 0.10over the same dates. They differ on 29 days, in November and December 2025 only. Both land in the same band. The heatmap and the window explorer below use the continuous account.

Why it was weak

Same machine, thinner margin per trade

Trade count, win rate, exit mix and market neutrality held. What changed is profit per trade: $5.48 net on average in the holdout run, so costs took about 48% of gross trading profit. That has happened before: 2021 ($2 a trade) and 2022 (−$1) were leaner, and the strategy recovered after both.
