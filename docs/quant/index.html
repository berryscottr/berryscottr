Market-neutral equity statistical arbitrage

Returns that don't ride the market, tested as if we expect to be doubted.

Every week the model scans the whole US stock market for pairs of companies whose prices move together, then trades their temporary divergences minute by minute: long one, short the other. It was built by statistics, not intuition, and checked with a one-shot holdout year that we committed to reading before we saw it.

What out-of-sample looks like

A real but modest edge, measured without flattery

The tuned period scored 1.49, validation 0.96, and the one-shot holdout year 0.16. Falling at each stage is what an honest edge looks like once tuning optimism wears off. One year of data has a standard error of about one Sharpe, so the holdout rules out a disaster but can't pin the edge down. That is why the next step is live trading, not more backtesting.

How we keep ourselves honest

Rules first, results second

4,699 configurations, all counted

Every setting ever tried is in a ledger, and the luck-adjusted (deflated) Sharpe is computed against that full count.

A holdout used once

The final year was locked away in code, run once for one frozen model, and read against bands committed beforehand.

Regions, not lucky points

A setting is accepted only on a plateau where every nearby configuration is profitable too.

Conservative costs

Slippage on every fill, regulatory fees, short-borrow fees and a 5% margin loan, never the cheapest broker assumption.

A null test

In a synthetic market with zero real pairs, 944 pairs passed every statistical screen. So the screen is a filter, never evidence.

Bugs reported, not buried

Errors we found ourselves are listed, with their effect on the numbers and the automated test that now guards each one.
